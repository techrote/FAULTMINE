# Silt Fault / #128 — selected from Estuary Moraine

**Editorial selection from actual native canonical pixels, 11 November 2026.** This is an artwork selection, not a proxy-image or source-only judgement.

## Observation

Of twelve native novelty-selected 384×240 results, descendant **128** best balances active corrosion with compositional legibility. Dense acid-green digital sediment creates a stippled terrain, traversed by three separated magenta/lime tidal contours. A broad, genuinely dark cobalt/black fissure breaks these contours and bends around a narrowing central tooth, while the right field resumes at a displaced phase. The fault remains a comprehensible spatial *event* rather than arbitrary hue replacement.

The green field is intentionally harsh and low-resolution. The scale is important: inspecting the actual canonical PNG at its native 384×240 showed the fine cellular texture and discontinuous contour bundles, not a smooth photographic effect.

## Rejected alternatives

- **#000:** red/copper field plus dark blue incision is striking but overwhelms the contour structure with uniform hot saturation; the editorial choice is not simply the first novelty sample.
- **#007/#048:** nearly featureless white and flat cyan fields; extreme distance in descriptor space does not make a finished composition.
- **#064:** bright scarlet smooth contour organization, but the palette flattens ground/figure separation.
- **#124:** high-frequency blue interference becomes a texture study, losing the readability of the shorelines.
- **#212/#243:** magenta speckle or blue granular noise dominates the line system.
- **#157/#230:** pale, low-contrast corrosion washes out the focal incision.

The remaining two finalists (#001 and #028) were considered in the 12-panel native sheet. This is a subjective selection, not an automated claim about mathematical quality.
